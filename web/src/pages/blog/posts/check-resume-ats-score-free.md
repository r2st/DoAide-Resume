## What is an ATS score?

There is no single official "ATS score". Different ATS products and online checkers calculate it differently. In practice, an ATS score is an estimate of two things:

1. **Parseability:** can the software read your resume correctly (name, contact details, job titles, dates, skills)?
2. **Match:** how closely your resume matches a specific job description in skills, keywords, job titles and experience.

That's why the same resume can score 85 for one job and 45 for another. A score without a job description only measures formatting.

## What is a good ATS score?

Because scoring varies between tools, treat these as rough guides rather than hard cutoffs:

| Score range | What it usually means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 80-100 | Strong match and clean formatting | Apply; tailor your summary |
| 60-79 | Decent, with missing keywords | Add relevant missing skills you genuinely have |
| 40-59 | Weak match or parsing problems | Fix formatting first, then keywords |
| Below 40 | Probably wrong role or unreadable file | Reconsider fit, or rebuild the resume |

Don't chase 100. A resume stuffed with keywords may pass software and then fail with the recruiter who reads it next.

## The 7 most common reasons for a low ATS score

### 1. Fancy templates

Two-column layouts, text boxes, icons and graphics can scramble the reading order. A clean single-column layout from [ATS-friendly templates](/templates) avoids this.

### 2. Contact details in the header or footer

Some parsers skip headers and footers. Put your name, phone and email in the main body.

### 3. Non-standard section names

"My Journey" or "Where I've Been" may not be recognised. Use standard headings: Summary, Skills, Experience, Projects, Education, Certifications.

### 4. Missing keywords from the job description

If the posting says "SQL, Power BI, stakeholder management" and your resume says "databases, dashboards, worked with teams", a keyword match will miss you. Use the employer's exact terms where they're true for you.

### 5. Skills only in a list, never in context

Some systems weigh skills that appear in your experience bullets as well as in a skills list. "Built Power BI dashboards for 12 regional managers" beats a bare "Power BI" entry.

### 6. Image-based or scanned PDF

A scanned PDF is an image. Many ATS cannot read it, or need OCR, which introduces errors.

### 7. Inconsistent dates and titles

Use one date format throughout (e.g. "Jun 2023 – Present") and real job titles.

2. **List the missing keywords** and mark the ones you genuinely have.

5. **Fix formatting flags:** one column, standard headings, no tables for layout.

## ATS tips for Indian job seekers

- **Campus and mass hiring:** large recruiters use online application portals that also parse resumes, so keep your file clean and skills explicit.
- **Naukri and LinkedIn:** your profile fields are searched separately from your resume file, so fill both. See our [Naukri resume format](/blog/naukri-resume-format-2026) guide.
- **Government jobs** usually use fixed application forms rather than resume parsing, so ATS optimisation matters less there.
- **Hindi or regional-language resumes:** most corporate ATS work best with English resumes. Keep a separate version for roles that require another language.
