Your LinkedIn profile already contains most of what a resume needs — your roles, dates, education, and skills. But LinkedIn's own "Save to PDF" produces a plain, unformatted document that looks nothing like a professional resume, and recruiters can tell instantly.

The better route: pull your LinkedIn data into a proper resume builder, pick a clean template, and tailor it per application. The [free LinkedIn resume import](/linkedin-resume-import) does exactly that, in your browser, in about two minutes.

## Why LinkedIn's "Save to PDF" isn't enough

- **No formatting control** — one fixed layout, no templates, no accent colors.
- **Everything is included** — recommendations, interests, and activity that don't belong on a resume.
- **Not tailored** — a resume should be adjusted for each job description; a profile export is one-size-fits-all.
- **ATS quirks** — the export's layout can scramble section order in some applicant tracking systems.

Use the export as a **data source**, not as the resume itself.

## What to rewrite after importing

A profile and a resume speak differently. After importing, spend ten minutes on these:

### 1. Turn your About section into a summary

LinkedIn About sections are conversational and written in first person. A resume summary should be 2–3 tight lines: role, years of experience, top skills, and one quantified achievement.

### 2. Convert duties into achievements

Profile entries often describe responsibilities ("Responsible for backend services"). Resume bullets should show outcomes: "Reduced API latency 40% by redesigning caching across 12 services."

### 3. Trim to the job description

Keep the 3–4 most relevant roles in detail; compress older ones to a single line. Mirror the key skills in the job posting — that's what both recruiters and [ATS scans](/ats-checker) look for.

### 4. Add what LinkedIn doesn't hold

Expected CTC-sensitive details stay off both, but a resume often needs things a profile lacks: a tailored headline, project links, and a skills section grouped by category.
