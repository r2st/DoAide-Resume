A large share of resumes rejected in India are never read by a human. An Applicant Tracking System (ATS) parses your resume into structured data — name, titles, skills, dates — and recruiters search and filter that data. If the parsing fails or the keywords don't match, your application sinks regardless of how good you are.

The good news: making a resume ATS-friendly is mechanical. Follow these 12 rules and you remove the software as an obstacle, in IT services and startups alike.

## Formatting rules (1–6)

### 1. Use a single-column layout

Multi-column templates look modern and parse terribly — an ATS reads across the page, interleaving your sidebar skills into your job descriptions. One column, top to bottom. Every template in the [free DoAide builder](/) is single-column for exactly this reason.

### 2. Use standard section headings

"Work Experience", "Education", "Skills", "Projects", "Certifications". The parser maps sections by these names; creative headings like "My Journey" or "What I Bring" can orphan an entire section.

### 3. No tables, text boxes, headers or footers

Content inside tables and text boxes is skipped or scrambled by many parsers, and some ignore the page header/footer entirely — so never put your phone number or email there. Keep contact details in the body, at the top.

### 4. Skip photos, icons and skill meters

Indian resumes historically carried photographs; modern Indian HR neither expects nor wants them, and an ATS cannot read them. Same for star ratings and progress bars on skills — write the skill as text, with proof in a bullet.

### 5. Export as a text-based PDF

A PDF exported from a builder or Word parses fine. A scanned document or image-based PDF does not. The test: open your PDF and try to select the text with your cursor. If you can't, an ATS can't read it either. Full details in our [ATS score guide](/blog/check-resume-ats-score-free).

### 6. Use standard fonts and dates

Arial, Calibri, Georgia, Helvetica — at 10.5–12pt. Write dates as "Jan 2023 – Mar 2025"; consistent, unambiguous date formats keep your experience calculation correct in portal filters.

## Keyword rules (7–10)

### 7. Mirror the job description's exact words

If the JD says "Accounts Payable" and your resume says "vendor payment processing", a keyword filter can miss you. Use the JD's terminology for the skills you genuinely have — recruiters search those exact phrases on Naukri and LinkedIn too (see our [Naukri resume format guide](/blog/naukri-resume-format-2026)).

### 8. Spell out abbreviations once

"Search Engine Optimization (SEO)", "Goods and Services Tax (GST) compliance". Recruiters search both forms; carrying both guarantees you match either.

### 9. Put a Skills section near the top

A dedicated skills block gives the parser an unambiguous list and gives the recruiter a 5-second overview. Group it (Languages / Frameworks / Tools, or Domain / Software) and keep every entry honest — Indian interviewers routinely pick a resume skill and drill into it.

### 10. Tailor for every application

The same resume scores differently against every job description. Before each application, run your resume and the JD through the [free ATS checker](/ats-checker), add the missing keywords you can honestly claim, and re-check. Ten minutes per application, and it compounds — our [ATS optimization guide](/blog/ats-optimization-2026) shows the full workflow.

## Content rules (11–12)

### 11. Quantify your bullet points

Parsing gets you seen; numbers get you shortlisted. "Handled client escalations" says nothing. "Resolved 30+ client escalations per month with 92% satisfaction" survives both the software and the skim. Two to four bullets per role, each starting with a verb and carrying a number where truthful.

### 12. Keep it to one page (freshers) or two (experienced)

Length doesn't break the ATS, but a recruiter's attention after the ATS is measured in seconds. Freshers should fit one page — our [fresher resume guide](/guides/fresher-resume) shows how to fill it without padding. Ten or more years of experience justifies two.

## The 5-minute ATS self-audit

1. Select-all in your PDF: does every word highlight?
2. Are your phone and email in the body, not a header?
3. Do your section headings match the standard five?
4. Copy the JD of a real opening and scan both in the [ATS checker](/ats-checker) — are the must-have keywords matched?
5. Does every skill you list have a bullet point backing it?

Pass all five and the software is no longer your problem — the interview is. For that, there's [interview preparation](/interview-preparation).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What makes a resume ATS-friendly?

Single-column layout, standard section headings, no tables or text boxes, text-based PDF export, standard fonts, and keywords that mirror the job description. The content stays yours; only the packaging changes.

### Do Indian companies really use ATS in 2026?

Yes. Large IT services companies, banks and most funded startups screen with ATS software or portal keyword filters, and recruiters on Naukri and LinkedIn search by keywords even when no formal ATS is involved. The same rules help in both cases.

### Is PDF or DOCX better for ATS in India?

A text-based PDF is safe for modern systems and preserves your formatting everywhere. If a portal explicitly asks for DOCX, provide it. Never upload a scanned or image PDF.

### How do I check if my resume is ATS-friendly for free?

Use a free checker: paste or upload your resume along with the job description, and review the parse and keyword report. The DoAide ATS checker is free, needs no signup, and shows matched and missing keywords per job.

### Can I use the same ATS-friendly resume for every job?

Keep one master resume, but adjust keywords and the summary for each application against its job description. Keyword match is calculated per job, so a resume that scores 85 for one role may score 50 for another.
